- Rows that miss design targets: the flood probe max (17.01 ms vs 10 ms) and
  flood typing p95 (23.6 ms vs 20 ms). The worst schedules sit in the fake vt
  (`Fake.Vt.put/2`, per-cell Elixir work for 15 x 100 MiB), which does not
  exist with the real kernel, and typing p95 includes the 6 ms frame pacing.
  Both are judged at M6. The numbers vary between full runs (earlier run:
  probe 11.34 ms, typing p95 22.7 ms; before the Compose fix: 23.1 ms and
  40.8 ms).
- The 15/1 frame ratio (7.93x) is not comparable until herdr's own ratio is
  measured; it was not.
- The idle rows (p95 ~420 us) wait for frames to go quiet before each key (see
  ruling on idle measurement). The "2ms after each frame" row is about 4.4 ms
  because Render paces frames at least 6 ms apart (`min_frame_interval_ms: 6`),
  so a key typed 2 ms after a frame waits roughly 4 ms for the next one. That
  is the 6 ms pacing floor, visible, not render cost.
- Before the final fix wave each keystroke produced two frames (rows, then a
  scene push for the cursor move), so fast typing paid pacing twice. The cursor
  now rides the row cache and a keystroke yields one frame; this is asserted by
  `keystroke_cost_test.exs` (1 key into a 15-pane 200x50 client: `count: 1`,
  `fetches: [typed]`, cursor at the right cell; the pre-fix lib gave `count: 2`
  and 15 fetches).

  40.8 ms. After the border-encoding fix (`Cells.repeat/3`): frame 81/591 us.

## Scene contract summary

Binding text is in the moduledocs of `HerdrScene.*`; this is the short form.

- `%Scene{size: %{cols, rows}, panes, chrome: Node, focus: %{pane, chrome}, cursor}`.
  `cursor` is the host-level cursor, filled for inline clients; ref clients derive
  it from the focused pane's cached cursor.
- `Scene.Pane{id, rect, border, rows, cursor, overlays, graphics}`. `rect` is the
  content rect (exactly the pane's grid). `border` is `%{rect, edges, title}` or
  nil. `rows` is `{:ref, pane_id, layout_gen}` or `{:inline, [{n, binary}]}`.
  `cursor` is `%{x, y, shape, visible}` relative to `rect`.
- `Scene.Node{role, id, rect, text, token, props, children}`. Roles: `:root :status
  :tabs :tab :text :button :divider :group :spacer :sidebar :modal`. Tokens:
  `:default :accent :muted :focus :warning :error :inverse`. No glyphs or colours;
  props are open (the mode and hint nodes carry structured `props`).
- `Scene.Overlay{kind, args}`: `:selection :dim :link_hover :scrollbar`, pane-relative.
- `HerdrScene.Event`: `{:key, %{key, mods, event, text}}`, `{:mouse, %{kind, button,
  x, y, mods, px}}` (x/y in scene cells), `{:paste, bin}`, `{:focus, bool}`,
  `{:resize, %{cols, rows, cell_px}}`, `{:chrome, path, action}`. Named keys
  `:enter :tab :backspace :escape :up :down :left :right :home :end :page_up
  :page_down :insert :delete :f1..:f12`, otherwise one grapheme. `mods` is a
  sorted, deduplicated subset of `[:alt, :caps_lock, :ctrl, :hyper, :meta,
  :num_lock, :shift, :super]`. Buttons: `:left :middle :right :none :wheel_up
  :wheel_down :wheel_left :wheel_right`.
- Protocol. Client to server: `{:attach, %{kind: :term | :gui, transport: :ref |
  :inline, size, cell_px, caps, client}}` via `GenServer.call(Protocol.endpoint(), _)`
  (`:herdr_client_endpoint`), reply `{:ok, session_pid, %{rows: {m, f, a}}}`
  (`validate_attach_reply/1`); `{:input, from, seq, event}`; `{:detach}`. Server to
  client: `{:scene, scene}`, `{:damage, pane_id, content_gen}`, `{:rows, pane_id,
  content_gen, [{n, bin}]}` (inline), `{:notice, %{level, text}}`, `{:ack, seq}`,
  `{:handoff, map}`.
- Rows MFA: `{HerdrServer.Pane.Rows, :fetch, []}`; the client calls
  `apply(m, f, [pane_id | a])` and gets `{gen, rows_tuple, cursor} | nil`, where
  `cursor` is the pane-relative `%{x, y, shape, visible}`. The row cache is ETS
  `:herdr_pane_rows`, objects `{pane_id, gen, rows, cursor}`, written by
  `Pane.Server` from `dirty_rows/1`, so clients never consume the vt's
  consume-on-read damage.
- Generations: `{:ref, id, gen}` carries the layout generation (bumps on resize);
  `{:damage}`/`{:rows}`/fetched `gen` carry the content generation (the vt damage
  gen). They are unrelated and clients must never compare them. A restarted
  `Pane.Server` does not bump the layout gen; it resubscribes, which forces full
  damage, so clients re-fetch.
- Fast path: `Store` publishes `:persistent_term.put(Protocol.fast_path_key(), %{gen,
  pane, write: {M,F,A} | nil, intercept: MapSet, passthrough})` when focus, mode,
  modal state or keybinds change. A key goes the fast path iff `passthrough and
  write != nil and {key, mods} not in intercept`, calling `Pane.Input.key/2`
  (encode_key + pty write) from the client's own process. After any slow-path
  event the client holds later events until its `{:ack, seq}`, so `"\x02v"` in
  one chunk splits correctly.
- Transport behaviour. Ref: the scene holds `{:ref, id, layout_gen}`; the client
  fetches rows by MFA and re-fetches only damaged panes, new panes and panes whose
  ref gen changed; ref scenes carry no cursors and damage does not re-push the
  scene. Inline: the Session fills every pane with its full rows and cursor on each
  scene push, sends `{:rows, ...}` on damage, and re-pushes the filled scene when
  the focused cursor moves (heavy, optimized in M16). Inline rows in a scene
  replace the pane's cache only when non-empty.

## Rulings

Contract decisions from the plan, then every ledger ruling in order
(`.superpowers/sdd/m3/progress.md`).

Plan contract decisions:

1. `Scene.Pane.rect` is the content rect; borders are scene data (`border`). The
   focused pane is `scene.focus.pane`. Cost if wrong: a GUI wanting the outer rect
   reads `border.rect`; changing the meaning later breaks fixtures and clients.
2. `{:ref, id, gen}` gen is the layout generation, not the damage generation, so
   scenes are not re-pushed on every damage. Cost if wrong: clients mix the two
   gens (documented as forbidden); fixing means re-pushing scenes per damage.
3. Rows come from a server-owned ETS row cache, not from `dirty_rows/1` by clients.
   Cost if wrong: two clients steal each other's damage; the cache costs one copy of
   each dirty row per damage.
4. PTY size follows the latest active client (tmux `window-size latest`); others
   clip or pad. Cost if wrong: another policy means changing the Store's driver step.
5. Client discovery is the registered name `:herdr_client_endpoint` from
   `Protocol.endpoint/0`. Cost if wrong: a remote transport needs a second discovery
   path; the name is one constant.
6. Fast path via `persistent_term` with the `passthrough`/`intercept` rule above.
   Cost if wrong: persistent_term updates are global-GC-heavy on change; they happen
   only on focus/mode/keybind changes (churn is a known gap, M6).
7. Packed cells are M1's `HerdrScene.Cell`; no provisional codec; slicing/joining
   only via `Cell.slice/3` and `concat/1`. Cost if wrong: none (binding instruction).
8. Golden fixtures are `inspect` output in `herdr_scene/priv/fixtures/scenes/*.exs`,
   regenerated with `HERDR_UPDATE_FIXTURES=1`. Cost if wrong: fixtures are plain
   data, convertible.
9. Property tests are hand-rolled (`:rand`, fixed seed, at least 1000 cases), no
   StreamData. Cost if wrong: no shrinking; add the dependency later.
10. Architecture test: `herdr_term` may not reference any `HerdrServer.*` atom.
    Cost if wrong: none; it is stricter than the milestone text.

Ledger rulings, in ledger order:

11. Task 2 defines `cursor/1` in the Vt behaviour and fake now (Task 7 consumes it).

## Deviations from design.md "NIF interfaces" (M1/M2 must know)

Behaviours are in `herdr_server/lib/herdr_server/kernel/{vt,pty,keeper}.ex` and
`herdr_term/lib/herdr_term/kernel/{tty,blit}.ex`.

- `Kernel.Vt.cursor(vt) :: %{x, y, shape, visible}`: new. `visible` from DECTCEM,
  `shape` from DECSCUSR (`:block | :underline | :bar`). Everything else follows
  design.md, including `mode/2 :: boolean | {:stale, boolean}`.
- `Kernel.Keeper` is new: `child_spec/1`, `open(pane_id, opts) :: {:ok, %{vt, pty,
  os_pid}} | {:error, term}`, `lookup(pane_id) :: {:ok, resources} | :error`,
  `close(pane_id) :: :ok`, `pane_ids() :: [id]`. `opts` is `%{cmd, args, env, cwd,
  cols, rows}`. `lookup/1` runs once per fast-path keystroke in the client's own
  process, so it must be callable from any process and must not serialize through
  one GenServer (a `read_concurrency` ETS table the keeper writes). The fake uses a
  `GenServer.call`, acceptable only for tests.
- Events must carry the pane id. `Native.Pty.spawn` in design.md has no id, so the
  Keeper injects it: `Kernel.Pty` events are `{:damage, id, gen}`, `{:exit, id,
  status}`, `{:title, id, bin}`, `{:bell, id}`, `{:osc, id, code, payload}`,
  `{:pwd, id, bin}` with `id` the pane id. The behaviour `spawn/7` takes `(vt, cmd,
  args, env, cwd, cols, rows)`; the fake also exposes `spawn/8` with the id first,
  which `Fake.Keeper` calls.
- `Kernel.Pty.subscribe/2` must force full damage on the vt (a late subscriber
  otherwise never sees the rows already there). `write/2` returns `:ok | {:error,
  :backpressure}`; `resize/4` takes `{w, h} | nil` cell px.
- `Fake.Tty.open/1` takes options for tests; the behaviour and design have
  `open/0`, and production passes no options. `Kernel.Tty` also has `restore/1`,
  `size/1 -> {cols, rows, px_w, px_h}`, `subscribe/2`, `write/2`, `suspend/1`;
  `Kernel.Blit.frame(tty, [{row, packed}], %{cursor, shape, repaint})`.
- Tolerances written into the server: `Pane.Server` logs a warning and keeps running
  on `{:error, _}` from `resize`, `set_hidden`, `subscribe`; `mode/2 {:stale, _}` is
  handled; `Input` has a `{:tty_resumed}` handler (forces a full repaint) with no
  producer yet.
- SIGWINCH: with a non-fake tty kernel, `Tty.Owner` calls
  `:os.set_signal(:sigwinch, :handle)` and installs a `gen_event` handler
  (`HerdrTerm.Tty.Sigwinch`) on `erl_signal_server`, which forwards `:sigwinch` to
  the owner. The NIF must not install signal handlers (design rule). With the fake
  no signal is touched; tests send `:sigwinch` to the owner.

## Swap procedure for the coordinator

1. Configuration lives in `live/config/config.exs`:
   `config :herdr_server, :kernel, vt:, pty:, keeper:` and
   `config :herdr_term, :kernel, tty:, blit:`. They are read with
   `Application.compile_env` in `HerdrServer.Kernel` and `HerdrTerm.Kernel`, so
   recompile both apps after changing them.
2. Point them at `HerdrServer.Native.Vt`/`Pty`/`Keeper` and
   `HerdrTerm.Native.Tty`/`Blit`. Each must implement the matching
   `HerdrServer.Kernel.{Vt,Pty,Keeper}` / `HerdrTerm.Kernel.{Tty,Blit}` behaviour:
   add `@behaviour`, or write thin adapters where the M1/M2 shapes differ (notably
   `Vt.cursor/1`, `Keeper`, pane id in `Pty` events).
3. Keep the fakes for `:test` and put the real modules in `:dev`/`:prod`, e.g.
   `if config_env() != :test, do: config :herdr_server, :kernel, vt: ...`
   (the fakes stay the defaults in `Kernel`'s `compile_env` fallbacks).
4. `Tty.Owner` decides signal wiring by checking whether the tty kernel is
   `HerdrTerm.Kernel.Fake.Tty`; no extra switch is needed.
5. Verify after the swap:
   - `Vt.cursor/1`: `visible` follows DECTCEM, `shape` follows DECSCUSR, and the
     cursor appears in `Pane.Rows` objects after output with no row damage.
   - `Keeper.lookup/1` is concurrent (no single-process bottleneck) under typing
     into many panes.
   - Pty events carry the pane id (title, bell, exit reach the right `Pane.Server`).
   - Backpressure: `Pty.write` returns `{:error, :backpressure}` instead of
     blocking; `Pane.Input` logs at most one warning per pane per second.
   - `cell_px` pass-through: the driving client's `cell_px` reaches `Vt.resize` and
     `Pty.resize`.
   - `subscribe` forces full damage; `mode/2` `{:stale, _}` handling.
   - Rerun the two manual exit checks and `just live-bench` against the real
     kernel (M6 judges the targets).
   - `scripts/attached_smoke.sh` now exercises `/dev/tty`. Its "terminal transcript
     empty" assertion will need revisiting because a real client writes to the tty.

`HerdrServer.SafeApply.run/4` and `HerdrTerm.SafeApply.run/4` are plain `try`

reason}`) and are the only blanket catches. Call sites (grep `SafeApply.run` under
`live/apps/*/lib`), so the swap is mechanical:

  (`Layout.panes/borders`), 299 (`Keybinds.intercept_set`).

init and sessions stop on its `:DOWN`.
